## Source-version conflict — MUST RESOLVE BEFORE TAX RULES
Two government-hosted copies of the Nigeria Tax Act, 2025, have DIFFERENT numbering around VAT:
- Federal Government Official Gazette reproduction (https://irs.gm.gov.ng/docs/national/NIGERIA_TAX_ACT_2025.pdf), inspected at PDF pp. 86, 88, 104–105 and 200, uses: **s148 VAT rate (7.5%)**, **s153 VAT invoice**, **s186 exemptions**, **s187 zero-rated**, Eleventh Schedule suspended/conditional supplies. Section 153(1) requires issuer Tax ID, serial invoice, supplier name/address/registration, supply date, buyer, gross and VAT/rate; s153(2) issuance upon supply even when unpaid, s153(4) possible directed e-invoicing.
- A National Assembly-hosted copy bearing the edited 1-1-2026 filename (https://nass.gov.ng/documents/download/11249), visually checked at PDF pp. 6–7, instead lists: **s147 VAT rate**, **s152 VAT invoice**, **s185 exempt**, **s186 zero-rated**, **s187 exemption by Presidential order**. The two MUST NOT be silently reconciled or mixed in code. Have Nigerian tax counsel confirm the controlling gazetted/amended/consolidated text, amendments, effective dates and formal modification orders before shipping a legal tax-category selector.
- Nigeria Tax Administration Act 2025 (https://nass.gov.ng/documents/download/11250), s23: electronic fiscal system obligation depends on NRS deploying/specifying a system and transition arrangement. Taxpayer eligibility/implementation phases cannot be inferred merely from UI preference. Official NRS-hosted text at https://www.nrs.gov.ng/uploads/NIGERIA_TAX_ADMINISTRATION_ACT_2025_8c945071a7.pdf (large PDF, retrieval limited).
- Government's 16 June 2026 announcement (https://fmino.gov.ng/fg-dismisses-reports-of-new-telecoms-fuel-taxes/) says petroleum VAT waiver remains. A 2026 VAT Modification Order committee was announced July 2026; the later enacted/issued order was NOT confirmed from the sources searched here. Do not infer that no later order exists. Check official gazette for each effective date before any hardcoding.

## Statutory invoice requirements / implications
1. Do not force a blanket 7.5% on every invoice. Determine VAT applicability by taxpayer + item + supply + valid law version.
2. Keep invoice display number OL-00001INV independent of official NRS IRN/QR and NRS business/service identifiers. Statutory sequential numbering of official documents must be server-allocated later, not browser.
3. Preserve merchant and customer supply snapshots and required tax fields when issuing. A quotation is not a VAT invoice and a paid receipt cannot invent tax.
4. Fiscalisation and delivery have separate states; NRS acceptance is not payment confirmation, and payment is not a filed VAT return.
5. Tax-sensitive edits after issuance require linked lawful adjustment/reversal, never mutate canonical issued snapshot.
